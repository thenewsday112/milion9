RP 349 : Quel avenir pour la ligne de la Maurienne ?

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

La ligne de la Maurienne relie Culoz à Modane, puis donne accès à l’Italie par le tunnel ferroviaire du Fréjus. Construite au XIXe siècle dans un relief accidenté, elle a nécessité la création de nombreux ouvrages d’art et est en forte rampe. Électrifiée dans un premier temps grâce à un troisième rail, particulier à cette ligne, elle a par la suite été modernisée. Après une période de prospérité, la concurrence de l’autoroute et des tunnels ferroviaires suisses a entraîné son déclin. Un éboulement majeur survenu le 27 août 2023 a interrompu le trafic jusqu’au 31 mars 2025. Son avenir pourrait bien dépendre de la mise en service du nouveau tunnel de la future ligne Lyon – Turin.

T. Pupier

Durée : 13 min

Railway Archives 1950: How the East Coast Main Line Recovered from the Devastating Floods of 1948

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The October 1950 issue of The Railway Magazine detailed the reconstruction work carried out following severe flooding on the East Coast main line between Berwick and Dunbar in August 1948. The report describes the replacement of seven bridges, repairs to a damaged culvert near Ayton and reconstruction work near Burnmouth.

Free Kirk Bridge over the Eye Water temporarily rebuilt as a viaduct consisting of military-type steel trestles and girders

The floods of August 1948

ON August 12, 1948, exceptionally severe floods caused serious damage on the East Coast main line between Berwick and Dunbar. The railway was closed completely for nearly 11 weeks, while temporary repairs were executed, and was reopened for goods traffic on October 25, and for passengers on November 1. Although the Great North Road runs at no great distance from the railway, difficulty was experienced in bringing materials and plant to some of the sites, and the preliminary work included the construction of special approach roads. The work of permanent reconstruction was put in hand in the autumn of 1948, and was finally completed on May 21 of this year with the erection of the 71 ft. 6 in. steel girders and concrete decking of bridge No. 130, near Reston, enabling the down track to be laid over it.

Replacing the damaged bridges

On the five-mile section between Grantshouse and Reston, seven bridges carrying the railway over the Eye Water were totally destroyed, and, as a temporary measure, steel bridges of a military type were built to replace them and to restore traffic as quickly as possible. They were erected in such a way that after the reopening of the line to traffic it would be possible to build concrete abutments and wing-walls as substructures for the permanent bridges without causing interference with traffic. This work progressed steadily according to schedule, and on February 19 this year work began on the placing of the new superstructures in position.

Six of these permanent superstructures are of the deck type, with one pair of steel girders under each line of railway, and the seventh is of the through type with a small depth of construction to give sufficient clearance between the river bed and the underside of the superstructure.

Temporary structure of the Free Kirk Bridge still in place after the concrete abutments and wings of the permanent bridge had been built

Working Sunday by Sunday

On each of the Sundays between February 19 and May 21 inclusive, with the exception of March 5 and April 9—or on twelve working Sundays in all—the half of each of the six deck-type superstructures, carrying either the up or down line, was replaced. The same procedure in the erection was followed each Sunday.

First, two locomotive cranes removed the girders and the top portions of the steel trestling forming the half of the temporary bridge carrying the up line. The two new steel girders to carry this track—which are of all-welded construction, and with diaphragms fixed in position weighed in all 26 tons—were next brought forward and placed in position. Special dished precast reinforced concrete units in 3-ft. widths were then set on top of the new girders to form a well-deck floor on which were laid the stone ballast and permanent way to carry the up line. On the following Sunday the other half of the temporary bridge was removed and the permanent superstructure erected to carry the down line, the transfer from temporary to permanent superstructures occupying two consecutive Sundays in the case of each bridge.

The seventh bridge

Meanwhile, the seventh bridge, already referred to as of the through type, had been replaced. The work entailing occupation of the line was carried out on the other two Sundays of this period. On March 5, in the first phase, the three main girders, also of welded construction, were placed by railway cranes on a prepared staging to one side of the temporary bridge and the steel joists to form the floor were set in place. On weekdays thereafter the steel joists were riveted to the main girders, and then encased in concrete to form the floor, which was waterproofed by covering with bituminous sheeting.

On Sunday, April 9, in the second phase, the line was completely cut—both up and down tracks being removed by a steam crane—and the new span, complete with deck or floor, and weighing about 350 tons, was slewed with the aid of hand winches into position over temporary runways of steel balls running between old rails. After being rolled into position, the span had to be lowered about 4 in. on to its bearings. This was accomplished by the use of sand-jacks—a simple arrangement of piston and cylinder—by which movements to very fine limits are obtained by the controlled release of sand from an opening in the base of the cylinder.

The Free Kirk Bridge reconstructed as a permanent concrete and steel deck structure carrying normal traffic

Reconstructing the culvert near Ayton

In addition to these bridges, the rehabilitation of the railway included the reconstruction of the culvert carrying the Horn Burn under the railway, near Ayton, where the flood waters had been impounded to a depth of some 26 ft. against the railway embankment. At this place, the clay in the 52-ft. embankment had been under water pressure for some weeks before the water level could be lowered and the burn diverted through a temporary culvert. As it was necessary to reconstruct the culvert close to its original position, the hazards of tunnelling through clay in an unstable condition, due to the absorption of water, had to be reduced by the application of electro-osmosis to dry out the material. In this work the Department of Scientific & Industrial Research gave much valuable assistance. The new culvert, 200 ft. long, was constructed with precast reinforced concrete segments, similar to those used in building the London tube railway tunnels, and is being lined internally with reinforced concrete placed in situ to give a finished internal diameter of 9 ft.

Repairs near Burnmouth

Another item in the 1948 flood damage was a slip which occurred in the embankment at milepost 53½, near Burnmouth. At this point the main line runs close to the sea, but about 200 ft. above the foreshore. As a temporary measure, the line was at first moved some 8 ft. to the landward side to permit the passage of trains at reduced speed. Boring operations here proved the presence of rock at reasonable depth below the railway formation, and mass concrete retaining walls and a bridge have now been constructed as a permanent repair. 400 yd. of new retaining wall built on the landward side has allowed the tracks to be re-aligned here for normal high-speed running.

Speed restrictions lifted

The speed restrictions which have operated since October, 1948, over the seven bridges, and between Burnmouth and Berwick-on-Tweed, were lifted when the summer timetable came into operation, and, later, when full speed is also permissible over the embankment at Ayton culvert, a chapter of events unprecedented in the history of railways in Britain will be brought to a close.

Jusqu’au 27 août 2027, Train World, le musée ferroviaire installé en gare de Schaerbeek, tout près de Bruxelles, propose une exposition temporaire sur le lien unissant mode et chemin de fer. Un évènement qui se déroule dans le cadre des 100 ans de la SNCB.

Arrivé dans la belle salle des guichets de l’ancienne gare de Schaerbeek, le visiteur découvre six créations contemporaines imaginées par de jeunes diplômés du College of Art and Design Brussels (CAD), une école supérieure bruxelloise de design. Ces jeunes talents ont été invités par la Société nationale des chemins de fer belges à créer les tenues des voyageurs qui empruntent ses trains. Dans le cadre du centenaire de la SNCB, célébré tout au long de l’année à travers une multitude d’évènements, Train World, le musée de la compagnie ferroviaire belge, propose aux visiteurs, depuis le 18 septembre, « Fashion & Trains. La mode au fil du rail, depuis 1835 ». La scénographie de cette exposition temporaire montre comment l’avènement du chemin de fer et son évolution technique ont influé sur la mode à travers la tenue des voyageurs, mais également celle des agents de la SNCB. Tout au long de la visite, des affiches, des objets et de nombreuses photos constituent une véritable « esthétique » de l’usage du train.

Le premier hall s’intéresse à l’époque de la naissance du chemin de fer belge en 1835 et à son rapide développement jusqu’au début du XXe siècle. Le rail devient alors un symbole ultime de la révolution industrielle et de la modernité triomphante. Tout au long de la deuxième partie du XIXe siècle, les tenues de voyageurs évoluent.

Dans le deuxième hall, c’est la mode de l’entre-deux-guerres qui assure le spectacle. Les tenues diffèrent parfois entre lignes locales, utilisées par les habitants de villages, et trains plus rapides reliant les villes, ou entre les déplacements professionnels et ceux dédiés aux loisirs. Cette dernière dichotomie est explorée dans le troisième hall. La démocratisation des voyages d’agrément influence également l’évolution des vêtements. Dans le quatrième hall, on monte à bord des trains de luxe internationaux et des luxueuses voitures de la famille royale belge de 1901 et 1939.

Robe d’été portée par la reine Fabiola en 1969. © Train World – SNCB

Tout au long du parcours d’exposition, l’évolution des uniformes des agents de la SNCB est mise en lumière, de la tenue d’apparat du chef de gare aux uniformes contemporains, en passant par les premiers uniformes féminins.

En admirant ces différents modèles, le visiteur se rappellera cette sentence définitive de la grande Coco Chanel : « La mode se démode, le style jamais ».

Llangollen Railway plans Remembrance Sunday Poppy Drop and free travel for veterans

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The Llangollen and Corwen Railway will mark Remembrance Sunday on 8 November 2026 with a special Poppy Drop led by two Royal Navy veterans, the temporary renaming of steam locomotive No. 3802 and free afternoon travel for Armed Forces personnel, veterans and cadets.

Mandy Towers lays the wreath on behalf of Llangollen and Corwen Railway

The Llangollen and Corwen Railway has announced a programme of commemorations for Remembrance Sunday, including two symbolic journeys through the Dee Valley, station tributes, wreath-laying ceremonies and a railway-wide act of remembrance.

Royal Navy veteran Garry Smith MBE and former Women’s Royal Naval Service (WRNS) member Ruth Smith will lead a special Poppy Drop, carrying poppies on separate train journeys between Llangollen and Corwen.

For the occasion, Great Western Railway heavy freight steam locomotive No. 3802 will be temporarily renamed ‘The Remembrance’ for the day.

The railway will also offer free travel on its afternoon services to serving Armed Forces personnel, veterans and members of recognised cadet organisations.

Remembrance Sunday services at Llangollen and Corwen

The railway’s usual 10am departure from Llangollen will be cancelled to allow staff and volunteers to attend Remembrance Sunday services and parades in their own communities.

At 11am, all activity at Llangollen Station will stop for the national Two-Minute Silence, observed by staff, volunteers and visitors.

Garry Smith MBE and Ruth Smith will represent the railway at the Remembrance Service at Llangollen War Memorial on Centenary Square, laying a wreath on its behalf.

At Corwen, Wayne Ronneback and the Corwen Station Team will represent the railway at the town’s Remembrance Service.

Mandy Towers was joined by Llangollen Railway Trust Manager David Hennigan with volunteers Mandy Towers, Glyn Jones and Mike Jackson The GWR Heavy Freight 3802, will be renamed ‘The Remembrance’ for the day. Mandy Towers lays the wreath on behalf of Llangollen and Corwen Railway Garry Smith MBE and his wife Ruth Smith will be leading Remembrance Day commerations in Llangollen.

Veterans to lead special Poppy Drop through the Dee Valley

Following the morning commemorations, Garry Smith MBE will travel on the footplate of the 12.20pm service from Llangollen to Corwen, releasing poppies on the approaches to stations along the route.

At Corwen, Ruth Smith will take over the tribute, travelling on the footplate of the 1.45pm return service to Llangollen and continuing the Poppy Drop.

The railway says the tribute will be planned and carried out in accordance with its operational and safety requirements.

Garry Smith MBE, a Royal Navy veteran, said:

“Remembrance Sunday is a deeply important occasion for all of us who have served in the Armed Forces. It is a time to remember our friends, our comrades and the generations of men and women who made the ultimate sacrifice.

“To have the honour of laying a wreath at Llangollen and then travelling along our beautiful railway, releasing poppies in remembrance, will be incredibly moving.

“Every poppy represents a life, a sacrifice and a family whose world was changed forever. As we travel through the Dee Valley, we will be remembering those who never came home.

“I am immensely proud to be part of this tribute and to represent the Llangollen and Corwen Railway.”

Ruth Smith, a former member of the Women’s Royal Naval Service, said:

“Remembrance is about more than a ceremony. It is about ensuring that the sacrifices made by previous generations are never forgotten.

“There is something especially poignant about Garry taking the poppies from Llangollen to Corwen and me bringing the tribute back along the line.

“Two journeys, travelling through the communities that our Railway serves, united by one message of remembrance.

“As a former Wren, it is a real privilege to take part in this special occasion. I hope everyone who sees the trains passing will take a moment to reflect on those who served, those who suffered and those who gave their lives for our freedom.”

GWR locomotive No. 3802 to carry ‘The Remembrance’ name

The railway’s Great Western Railway heavy freight steam locomotive No. 3802 will be temporarily renamed ‘The Remembrance’ for Remembrance Sunday.

The name will be carried for the day as a tribute to the service and sacrifice of generations of Armed Forces personnel.

Remembrance displays at Glyndyfrdwy and along the railway

Volunteers at Glyndyfrdwy Railway Station have prepared a Remembrance display featuring two silhouettes representing members of the Armed Forces, accompanied by flags.

Flags and poppies will also be displayed at every station along the Llangollen and Corwen Railway.

The railway hopes these displays will encourage passengers, visitors and local residents to reflect on the sacrifices made by servicemen and women.

Free train travel for Armed Forces personnel, veterans and cadets

The railway will offer free travel on all its Remembrance Sunday afternoon services to serving Armed Forces personnel, veterans and members of recognised cadet organisations.

The offer includes personnel serving in the Royal Navy, British Army and Royal Air Force, former service personnel, and members of the Sea Cadets, Army Cadets and RAF Air Cadets, including the Air Training Corps.

Those wishing to travel free of charge must present appropriate proof of service or cadet membership at one of the railway’s ticket offices.

Accepted identification includes a military identification card, Veterans Railcard, Veterans ID Card or recognised cadet identification.

The complimentary travel offer is intended as a gesture of thanks to serving personnel, veterans and young people involved in cadet organisations.

Remembrance Sunday train timetable – 8 November 2026

The railway has confirmed the following afternoon services:

Llangollen to Corwen:

  • 12.20pm
  • 3.15pm

Corwen to Llangollen:

  • 1.45pm
  • 4.20pm

There will be no 10am departure from Llangollen, allowing railway staff and volunteers to attend local Remembrance Sunday commemorations.

The commemorations will bring together railway staff, volunteers, veterans, cadets and the communities of Llangollen and Corwen.

Through the wreath-laying ceremonies, Two-Minute Silence, special station displays, the temporary renaming of locomotive No. 3802 and the Poppy Drop journeys led by Garry and Ruth Smith, the railway aims to provide opportunities for remembrance along the heritage line.

The Llangollen and Corwen Railway hopes passengers, visitors and local residents will join in remembering those who made the ultimate sacrifice.

Au cœur des Pyrénées, la monumentale gare de Canfranc, inaugurée en 1928 mais exploitée pendant cinquante ans seulement, connaît une seconde vie. Un livre vient de lui être consacré. Présentation.

L’une est professeur d’histoire-géographie, l’autre est géographe et président de l’association Mémoire d’Aspe. Régine Péhau-Gerbet et Jean-Luc Palacio ont écrit à quatre mains un ouvrage inédit, illustré de photos en couleur, consacré à la gare internationale de Canfranc, qui fut inaugurée en 1928 après des années de travaux au cœur des Pyrénées, en Aragon, à la frontière franco-espagnole. Qualifiée par les auteurs de « joyau architectural », Canfranc a vu son destin particulièrement entravé. « Elle incarne une épopée ferroviaire hors norme. Entre 1908 et 1928 dans ce coin perdu de l’Aragon, un chantier titanesque a transformé Los Arañones, un minuscule hameau isolé, situé à 1 200 mètres d’altitude, en une cité ferroviaire moderne », explique le duo. « Avec ce livre, nous avons voulu retracer les grandes étapes de cette aventure technologique et humaine, du cheminement du projet à la construction de la gare, de son exploitation mouvementée à la perte de son statut international jusqu’à sa renaissance récente. »

Le Titanic des Pyrénées

A elles seules, les dimensions de l’édifice monumental stupéfient le visiteur. L’impression se prolonge une fois rentré à l’intérieur. La hauteur sous plafond – plusieurs dizaines de mètres – de l’ex-bâtiment voyageurs évoque une cathédrale laïque. « Cette gare impressionne encore de nos jours », confirment Régine Péhau-Gerbet et Jean-Luc Palacio. « L’Espagne souhaitait être reliée au reste de l’Europe et ce site était le seul dimensionné pour y bâtir une gare internationale aussi monumentale. »

Las, Canfranc n’a jamais atteint tout son potentiel en tant que gare frontière entre les deux pays. « On recense moins de cinquante ans d’exploitation, pour de multiples raisons : la crise de 1929, la guerre civile en Espagne, la Seconde Guerre mondiale, le déraillement meurtrier d’un train de fret, dans les années 1970, côté français sur la ligne Bedous – Canfranc, ce qui a entraîné la fermeture de la ligne côté français. Tombée dans l’oubli, la gare a été surnommée le “Titanic des Pyrénées”, véritable naufrage ferroviaire. »

La façade de la gare internationale de Canfranc. © Anne Jeantet-Leclerc/Photorail/LVDR

A l’extérieur, une partie des voies ont été déferrées et ont cédé la place à un vaste parc public qui intègre des vestiges ferroviaires préservés et réutilisés : les anciennes portes de la gare marquent désormais l’entrée du parc. En 2021, les halles marchandises ont été transformées en une nouvelle gare voyageurs.

Transformé en hôtel de luxe

Encore un été de grands chantiers en Île-de-France

La RATP poursuit la régénération de la ligne. Après la zone ouest traitée entre 1993 et 1998 sur 21 km puis le tronçon entre 2015 et 2023 pour 23 km, le chantier aborde désormais les branches de la partie est, soit 80 km entre Vincennes et Boissy-Saint-Léger d’une part, Marne-la-Vallée-Chessy d’autre part. Du 8 au 23 août, la ligne a donc été fermée entre Vincennes et Noisy-le-Grand-Mont-d’Est pour les voies (rails, traverses et ballast) et des appareils de voie.

Par ailleurs, pour sa rénovation (étanchéité, quais, accès), la gare de Nation a été fermée du 29 juin au 30 août avec passage de trains sans arrêt. Il s’agit d’une première étape d’un chantier qui se poursuivra jusqu’en 2030.

Enfin, en gare de Val-de-Fontenay, un nouveau passage souterrain piéton a été mis en place. Plus grand (17 m de long, 11 m de large), le caisson de 80 t a été mis en place le week-end du 8-9 août. Il facilitera les flux de voyageurs entre les RER A et E en attendant le T 1, la ligne 15 est et peut-être un jour la ligne 1 du métro.

Alors que cette ligne est très chargée même en été avec les touristes se rendant par exemple à Disneyland Paris, des bus de substitution allongeaient les temps de trajet.

En 2027, le chantier sera situé entre La Varenne-Chennevières et Boissy-Saint-Léger.

États-Unis : les coulisses du métro de New York vues par Christopher Payne

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

La Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA) a annoncé le 22 septembre dernier l’ouverture de « In Service: MTA Photographs by Christopher Payne », l’exposition d’une série de 50 photographies qui offre aux usagers un regard rare sur les ateliers de maintenance et les installations industrielles qui font fonctionner le réseau.

Christopher Payne, photographe spécialisé dans les sujets industriels et architecturaux, a visité treize installations et usines réparties dans quatre États. Il s’est également concentré sur les travaux d’infrastructure invisibles du grand public et sur les savoir-faire spécialisés nécessaires pour construire et entretenir les machines.

Le projet s’inspire de la série MTA Overhaul Shops, réalisée par Christopher Payne dans deux ateliers de maintenance, et publiée dans un article du New York Times en 2024. Dix photographies de cette série servent de socle à l’exposition. Les 40 autres images ont été commandées par MTA Arts & Design et réalisées entre mai et août 2026.

Le photographe a documenté le travail quotidien de maintenance dans chacune des divisions de l’agence. Il a également parcouru le pays pour photographier la construction de matériel roulant. Il s’est notamment rendu sur les sites d’assemblage des rames de métro R211 de Kawasaki, à Yonkers (New York) et à Lincoln (Nebraska), sur les sites de production de bus articulés de New Flyer, à St. Cloud (Minnesota) et à Jamestown (New York), ainsi que dans l’usine de locomotives de Siemens à Sacramento (Californie).

Depuis près de quarante ans, MTA Arts & Design transforme d’anciens caissons publicitaires désaffectés en galeries d’art. Cette fois, les images de la série In Service occuperont tous les caissons lumineux de cinq grandes stations (42 St-Bryant Park, Atlantic Av-Barclays Ctr, Bowling Green, Grand Central Terminal, Dining Concourse et Grand Central Madison) pendant six mois.
